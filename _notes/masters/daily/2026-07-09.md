in general it's a couple of distinct ideas and I'm chaotically moving between them
starting from a [[RAVE]]-style audio VAE:
- Q: does reducing the latent space to a single vector allow for similar reconstruction quality?
    - A: yes
- Q: does reducing the latent space to a single vector allow for similar unconditional generation quality with a less complicated prior?
    - A: yes
- Q: do NFs improve reconstruction/generation for the single-latent VAE?
    - A: same reconstruction/generation results for the NF posterior, but the NF prior improves generation quality and diversity considerably
- Q: does a single-latent VAE capture disentangled global properties in the latent space?
    - A: some, but most don't really seem linearly extractable
- Q: does an audio VAE like this work on tougher types of data?
    - A: to be determined
- Q: is a single-latent VAE comparable with other methods that generate short audio samples?
    - A: to be determined

and if I were to formulate a single hypothesis?
```
An audio VAE based on RAVE but with no time-step dimension for the latent representation can match the reconstruction/unconditional generation quality of RAVE as well as other works doing short musical audio sample generation. Additionally, such an architecture captures global properties of the sound in its latent space, enabling controllability of the generated sound.

side hypothesis: using NFs for the posterior/prior improves the quality of such a single-latent VAE
```
